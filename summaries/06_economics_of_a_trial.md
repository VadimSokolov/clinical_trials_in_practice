This chapter asks what clinical trials cost, who pays, and how those economics shape drug development. It reports estimates of what it costs to develop a drug through approval (the 2025 Deloitte cohort average is $2.67 billion), critiques of the Tufts estimate, costs by phase, and the cost of a day of delay. It follows money from sponsors to contract research organizations (CROs), sites and vendors, and covers site budgets, patient out of pocket costs, trial globalization and the rise of China. It also explains how artificial intelligence and automation can change cost structure, funding interim analyses, predictive probability of success (PPoS), assurance as against power, and platform trial economics. A reader can compare cost benchmarks with their assumptions, build a site budget, specify a funding checkpoint, and judge a program on assurance rather than power.
