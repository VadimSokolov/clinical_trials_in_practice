This chapter asks how trials can learn from accumulating data while keeping Type I error under control, and why fixed designs strain under precision medicine, rare diseases and many treatment options. It builds from the bandit problem (Thompson sampling, dynamic programming) and Bayesian updating with Beta priors, through early Bayesian adaptive trials (CALGB 49907, ASTIN), Phase II/III dose selection (AWARD-5), an adaptive Phase II trial (lecanemab) and a platform trial (I-SPY 2), to hierarchical models that borrow information across tumor types in basket trials. It also covers predictive probabilities, administrative analyses, simulation of operating characteristics, Type I error in Bayesian trials, response adaptive randomization, and guidance from the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), including the 2026 Bayesian draft, draft E20 and the one trial standard. A reader can judge where response adaptive randomization pays and what simulation a regulator expects.
