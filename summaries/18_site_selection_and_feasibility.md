How does a sponsor find, assess and activate the clinical research sites that will actually enroll patients? The chapter covers site identification (access to patients, geographic distribution, prior experience), the falling number of U.S. investigators and the one and done pattern, and Diversity Action Plans under the Food and Drug Omnibus Reform Act (FDORA) of 2022, whose guidance from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has been in flux. It presents the feasibility assessment with its criteria and red flags, the tendency of sites to overestimate enrollment, and the Clinical Trial Agreement (CTA), site budget and coverage analysis that stand between a qualified and an activated site. It reports 2024 WCG survey data on startup bottlenecks by site type, then the site initiation visit (SIV) and global trials. A reader can screen candidate sites against explicit criteria, discount investigator enrollment estimates, and plan contracts and budgets.

- Enhancing Participation in Clinical Trials, final guidance (FDA 2025)
- Roughly 11% of sites enroll no patient and another 37% under enroll (Tufts Center for the Study of Drug Development 2013; Getz 2012)
- Site activation and enrollment benchmarks for 2012, 2019 and 2023 (Lamberti et al. 2024)
- Physician survey on reasons for not serving as investigators (Harris Interactive 2004)
- Discounting site enrollment predictions (Getz 2008)
- Cost to bring an asset to launch, failures included, $2.6 to 2.7 billion (Deloitte Centre for Health Solutions 2026; DiMasi et al. 2016)
