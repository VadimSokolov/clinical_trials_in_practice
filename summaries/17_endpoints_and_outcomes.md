Which outcome should a trial measure to demonstrate that a drug works, and how does that choice shape design, sample size and regulatory strategy? The chapter sets out the hierarchy of clinical endpoints, surrogate endpoints and biomarkers, including overall survival, progression free survival, patient reported outcomes (PROs) and their validation, and digital health technology (DHT) endpoints. It shows how surrogates can mislead (the Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial, CAST, and hormone therapy), how reasonably likely surrogates can support accelerated approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for serious conditions, and how minimal residual disease (MRD) and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) are being studied. It covers primary and secondary endpoints, multiplicity, composite and time to event endpoints, ordinal endpoints, the win ratio, desirability of outcome ranking (DOOR), and the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E9(R1) estimands framework with its strategies for intercurrent events such as death. A reader can choose an endpoint and state its estimand.

## From the literature

- Estimands and strategies for intercurrent events (ICH E9(R1) 2019)
- Hormone therapy raised cardiovascular events despite favorable lipid effects (Rossouw et al. 2002)
- CAST: arrhythmia suppression with higher mortality (Echt et al. 1991)
- Win ratio (Pocock et al. 2012)
- Desirability of outcome ranking, DOOR (Evans et al. 2015; Evans and Follmann 2016)
- Ordinal outcomes: modified Rankin Scale (Saver 2007) and proportional odds model (Agresti 2010)
- World Health Organization (WHO) Clinical Progression Scale for COVID-19 (WHO Working Group 2020)
- FDA guidance on cancer endpoints, outcome assessments and digital health technologies (FDA 2018; FDA 2023)
- MRD as an accelerated approval endpoint in myeloma (FDA Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee 2024; Landgren and Devlin 2025)
