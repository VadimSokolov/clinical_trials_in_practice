This chapter asks when real world data can support regulatory and payer decisions as real world evidence (RWE), and how to design and analyze such studies. It covers the United States framework (the 21st Century Cures Act, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) 2018 framework, fit for purpose), the European Medicines Agency's DARWIN EU, health technology assessment and managed entry agreements, and data sources (electronic health records, claims, registries, Sentinel). Designs include external and synthetic control arms, hybrid randomized designs with power, meta analytic predictive (MAP) or commensurate priors, registry based randomized trials and embedded pragmatic trials. Methods include the active comparator new user design, immortal time bias, propensity scores, instrumental variables, difference in differences, regression discontinuity, E-values and negative controls. Cases include palbociclib in male breast cancer, where FDA treated the real world data as supportive rather than primary, and COVID-19 vaccine effectiveness. A reader can decide when RWE belongs in a development program.
