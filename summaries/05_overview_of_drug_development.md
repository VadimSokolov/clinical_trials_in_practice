This chapter answers how a compound moves from discovery to an approved medicine, and why most compounds that reach human testing do not complete the path. It follows preclinical research, the Investigational New Drug (IND) filing with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the four clinical phases, and regulatory review. It sets Phase I to IV beside the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E8(R1) classification of studies by objective and its Critical to Quality (CtQ) factors. It covers expedited programs (Fast Track, Breakthrough Therapy, Accelerated Approval, Priority Review), the Target Product Profile (TPP), and attrition: about 28% of programs pass from Phase II to Phase III, and about 7% of programs starting Phase I reach the market (2020 to 2024 averages). A reader can place a study in the phase and objective framework, see which expedited programs can be combined, and read stage success rates when judging risk.
