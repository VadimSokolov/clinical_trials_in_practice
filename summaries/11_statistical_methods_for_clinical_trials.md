This chapter explains how a confirmatory trial separates a modest treatment effect from large uncontrolled variation, and which frequentist methods support the comparison. It covers hypothesis testing, Type I error, power and sample size; intent to treat and the estimands framework of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E9(R1); survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier, log-rank, Cox, restricted mean survival time, competing risks); analysis of covariance and mixed models for repeated measures (MMRM); missing data (missing at random versus not at random, multiple imputation, tipping point analysis); group sequential designs with alpha spending, futility rules and the promising zone design; sequential multiple assignment randomized trials (SMART) for dynamic treatment regimes; and causal inference from observational data (propensity scores, target trial emulation, marginal structural models, E-values). A reader can choose an estimand and a missing data strategy before enrollment, allocate alpha across interim looks, and judge whether a SMART is justified.
