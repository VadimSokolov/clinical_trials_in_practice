This chapter explains what a clinical study report (CSR) is, who reads it, how it is written and how its results should be reported. It presents the structure of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E3 guideline with typical page lengths, and the writing process: medical writers working from the statistical analysis plan, the locked database and tables, listings and figures (TLFs), early drafting of a shell, and when an abbreviated CSR is acceptable. It covers disclosure: the summary of results for laypersons under EU Clinical Trials Regulation 536/2014, European Medicines Agency (EMA) Policy 0070 with its anonymization report, and the oseltamivir (Tamiflu) reanalysis based on full CSRs. It then covers reporting against the prespecified estimand under ICH E9(R1), Bayesian results as posterior probabilities with credible intervals (the REBYOTA review is the example), communicating uncertainty, and adaptive and platform trial reports, and places the CSR in Module 5 of the Common Technical Document. A reader can plan a CSR and judge whether an abbreviated report applies.
