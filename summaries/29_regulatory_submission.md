This chapter explains how a sponsor builds, submits and defends a marketing application, mainly in the United States, and how other regions differ. It defines the New Drug Application (NDA), Biologics License Application (BLA), Abbreviated New Drug Application (ANDA), Marketing Authorization Application (MAA) and the Common Technical Document (CTD) with its five modules. It covers FDA meeting types, expedited programs, the 2022 Food and Drug Omnibus Reform Act (FDORA) changes to accelerated approval, and the review cycle: refuse to file decisions, Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) goals, advisory committees, Bioresearch Monitoring (BIMO) inspections and complete response letters. A long section examines approval on a single trial: the 0.025 squared false positive probability behind the two trial rule, Fisher's method, the 1997 Food and Drug Administration Modernization Act (FDAMA) exception, five supporting features, the loss of breadth and the June 2026 revised draft guidance. It closes with the European, Japanese and Chinese pathways and postmarketing obligations. A reader can choose a pathway and judge whether one pivotal trial could support approval.

- One pivotal trial as the new FDA default (Prasad and Makary 2026)
- Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 (European Parliament and Council 2004)
- ICH E5(R1) bridging studies (ICH 1998) and ICH E17 multiregional trials (ICH 2017)
- ICH M4, Common Technical Document (ICH 2016), and ICH M8, eCTD (ICH 2015)
- Real-Time Oncology Review, piloted 2018, and Project Orbis, launched 2019 (FDA Oncology Center of Excellence 2024)
