This chapter explains how a first human dose is chosen and what Phase I studies measure. It covers starting dose selection from the NOAEL (no observed adverse effect level), reduced by a default safety factor of 10, and from the MABEL (minimal anticipated biological effect level), with the 2006 TGN1412 case as the warning. It covers sentinel dosing and stopping rules for first in human trials, single and multiple ascending dose studies, pharmacokinetic parameters, and special studies such as bioequivalence and thorough QT. For oncology it compares the 3+3 design with BOIN (Bayesian optimal interval), CRM (continual reassessment method) and BLRM (Bayesian logistic regression model), adds EWOC (escalation with overdose control), and explains Project Optimus and combination escalation. A reader can choose a starting dose framework, write dose limiting toxicity definitions and stopping rules, and simulate an escalation design before the trial begins.
