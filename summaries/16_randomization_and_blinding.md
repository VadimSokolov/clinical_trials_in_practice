How do randomization and blinding protect a trial from bias, and which methods suit which trials? The chapter compares simple, block, stratified block, minimization (dynamic allocation), response adaptive randomization (RAR), covariate adaptive and cluster randomization by information needed, balance and predictability. It explains allocation concealment, interactive response technology (IRT), treatment crossover, and the blinding provisions of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E6(R3) guideline. It covers open label, single blind, double blind and triple blind designs, side effects that reveal assignment, and sham procedures and active comparators for device and behavioral trials. The randomized play the winner rule, Thompson sampling and the neonatal extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) trials illustrate RAR, for which equal randomization is generally preferred in confirmatory Phase III trials with two arms. A reader can choose an allocation method, plan concealment and blinding, and judge when adaptive allocation is defensible.

## From the literature

- Randomized trials of hormone therapy contradicted observational studies (Rossouw et al. 2002)
- Purposes of randomization: bias avoidance, error estimation, exact tests (Cox 2009)
- Biased coin design (Efron 1971)
- Minimization, or dynamic allocation (Taves 1974; Pocock and Simon 1975; Scott et al. 2002; Coart et al. 2023)
- Subverting allocation through sealed envelopes (Schulz 1995)
- Randomized play the winner rule (Wei and Durham 1978)
- Neonatal ECMO trials (Bartlett et al. 1985; UK Collaborative ECMO Trial Group 1996)
- Equal randomization generally preferred to RAR in two arm Phase III trials (Korn and Freidlin 2011; Proschan and Evans 2020)
- Covariate adaptive randomization (Atkinson 2002; Lin, Zhu and Su 2015) and covariate adjustment guidance (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2023)
- Textbooks on randomization (Rosenberger and Lachin 2016; Hu and Rosenberger 2006)
