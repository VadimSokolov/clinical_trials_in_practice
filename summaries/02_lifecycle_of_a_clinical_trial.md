What stages does a clinical trial pass through, and where does it sit in drug development? The chapter maps the sequence from discovery and preclinical testing through the Investigational New Drug (IND) application or Clinical Trial Application (CTA), Phase I to Phase IV, submission, review and approval. It tabulates the question, typical size, duration and key decisions of each phase, and reports likelihood of approval from Phase I in a Biotechnology Innovation Organization analysis of 2011 to 2020 programs (oncology 5.3%, programs outside oncology 9.3%). It presents the 4DM (Drug Discovery, Development and Deployment Map) as an alternative to the linear pipeline. It then follows one Phase III trial through planning and design, startup, execution and closeout, up to the Clinical Study Report (CSR) and a Marketing Authorization Application (MAA), New Drug Application (NDA) or Biologics License Application (BLA), and names four cross cutting concerns: regulatory compliance, ethics, quality and data integrity (the ALCOA principles: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate), and economic constraints. A reader can place the main operational tasks of a trial in their stage and find the book chapter that treats each.
