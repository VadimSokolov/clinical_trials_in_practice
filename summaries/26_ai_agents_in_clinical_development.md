This chapter asks where artificial intelligence (AI) agents can be used in clinical development and what regulators require first. It treats agentic AI as a design pattern of compound systems (models plus retrieval, tools and control logic) and reviews building blocks and failure modes. The regulatory section covers the Guiding Principles of Good AI Practice of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA), FDA's draft seven step credibility assessment, the EMA reflection paper, model lifecycle maintenance and the EU AI Act. It maps AI uses in protocol design, recruitment, data quality and logistics, and treats federated learning, digital twins (PROCOVA), real world evidence, external controls and synthetic control arms. The economics section sets out cost drivers and an illustrative 2030 scenario. A reader can place an AI use in a risk tier and decide what validation and regulator contact it needs.
