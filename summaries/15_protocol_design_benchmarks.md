How large and complex are clinical trial protocols, how has that changed, and what does complexity cost? The chapter reports Tufts Center for the Study of Drug Development (Tufts CSDD) benchmarks by phase, for oncology against other protocols, and for rare disease against other protocols: endpoints, eligibility criteria, procedures, visits, countries, sites, datapoints, completion rate, and time from protocol finalization to database lock. It separates core procedures (those that serve a primary or key secondary endpoint) from noncore ones, reports that heavier protocols tend to show lower recruitment, retention and adherence and more deviations and amendments, and lists drivers of complexity. It separates protocol completion from loss of outcome follow up, which sample size planning allows for, and links the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E6(R3) guideline to proportionate trial processes. The data predate the pandemic and are best read as a floor. A reader can compare a draft protocol with these benchmarks and decide which noncore procedures to challenge.

## From the literature

- Phase III averages 116 pages, 266 procedures and 1,328 days from finalization to database lock (Getz et al. 2023)
- Oncology against other protocols: 315 against 243 procedures, completion 31% against 80% (Getz et al. 2023)
- Rare disease against other protocols: enrollment 603 against 444 days (Getz et al. 2023)
- Nearly a quarter of Phase II and III procedures are noncore (Getz et al. 2023)
- More endpoints, criteria and procedures tend to track worse performance (Getz et al. 2023)
- Sample size planning allows for loss of outcome follow up (ICH E9(R1) 2019)
- Factors critical to quality and proportionate trial processes (ICH E6(R3) 2025)
