Why do clinical trials follow the rules they do? The chapter links the main protections of modern trials to the events that preceded them: the Elixir Sulfanilamide deaths and the 1938 Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, the Nuremberg Code, thalidomide and the 1962 Kefauver-Harris Drug Amendments, the Declaration of Helsinki, the Tuskegee Syphilis Study, and the Belmont Report with the Common Rule. It then follows the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) and its Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guideline E6 through the R2 and R3 revisions, and records recent developments, mainly Food and Drug Administration (FDA) guidance on decentralized trials, adaptive and Bayesian designs, real world evidence, master protocols and Diversity Action Plans, plus the start of the EU Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS). A reader can match each protection (safety testing of drugs, voluntary consent, independent review by an institutional review board, proof of efficacy) to its event and see which current guidance is final and which is a draft.
