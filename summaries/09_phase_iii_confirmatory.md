This chapter explains what Phase III confirmatory trials must show and how they are designed, monitored and justified for approval. It compares Phase II and Phase III trials, and covers the choice of control (placebo or active, superiority or noninferiority), the estimand and its five attributes under the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E9(R1), and the statistical analysis plan. It explains interim analyses and group sequential designs (O'Brien-Fleming, Pocock and Lan-DeMets boundaries), futility stopping, the two pivotal trial paradigm and the single trial standard, and designs that join Phase II and III in one trial. It covers ICH E1 safety exposure for long term treatment (about 1500 patients), Data Safety Monitoring Boards (DSMBs), ICH E5 bridging studies, and Diversity Action Plans. A reader can choose a control and estimand, plan interim looks without inflating Type I error, and judge when one pivotal trial may suffice.
